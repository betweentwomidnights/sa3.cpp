How the lanes are blended. The defaults are a good place to start; these are here to experiment with. Some background: the model writes the whole clip at once, starting from pure noise and refining it over the steps (8 by default). The first few steps decide the arrangement (groove, melody, where things happen); the last ones polish texture and detail. "σ" is how noisy the clip still is: 1 = pure noise, 0 = finished.

Velocity (default) runs every lane as its own complete pass, then blends them moment by moment using your curves. It is precise: a lane drawn at 0 really is off there. Each lane adds one pass, so more lanes take longer.
Layer is the original single-pass method, kept for comparison. It is cheaper, but the LoRA bleeds across the whole clip (a lane at 0 still measured 60-80% LoRA). One lane only, no lane prompt.

Every moment of the clip listens to every other moment, so sections tend to drift towards one shared sound and a LoRA can get washed out. Isolation makes each moment listen mostly to moments with a similar lane mix while the arrangement is being decided, so each phase keeps its own identity. Higher = more separation, but also more of a "changing radio stations" jump at transitions. 0 = off. Around 3 is already full strength; above that changes little.

When isolation lets go. After that, every moment listens to the whole clip again, which smooths the joins. 0.9 = isolate the first 4 of 8 steps. Closer to 1 = shorter isolation (in tests, fewer than the first 4 steps made no difference at all); lower = isolate longer, leaving fewer steps to smooth the transitions.

The opposite trade to isolation. While σ is above this, every lane plays at its average amount across the whole clip, so the take settles on one arrangement first; your curves only take over afterwards, shaping sound and texture more than the song itself. In theory: smoother, more "same song" transitions, possibly with less contrast between phases. Isolation pauses while this is active. 0 = off; try 0.9 with isolation 0.

What the lanes do
Each lane is its own DiT pass per step (its LoRA at the curve's peak, on its own prompt if set). The base pass runs on the base prompt; at each frame the step is base + Σ amount × (lane − base). Amount is a strength multiplier: 1 = the LoRA at its strength. The blending options are under Advanced.

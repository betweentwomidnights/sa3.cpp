How the lanes blend. The defaults keep a take sounding like one song while your curves shape it. The model refines the whole clip from noise over the steps: early steps decide the arrangement (groove, melody, where things happen), and later steps decide sound and texture. σ is the noise left in the clip, from 1 at the start to 0 when it's finished.

Every lane plays at its average level until σ falls to this value, so the arrangement forms from one mix. Then your curves take over. Higher hands over sooner. 0 skips this, and your curves apply from the first step.

Moments in a clip listen to each other, which pulls sections toward one shared sound and can wash out a LoRA. Isolation makes each moment listen mostly to moments with a similar lane mix, so sections keep their own character. 0 is off and 1 is gentle. Around 3 is full strength, where transitions can jump like changing radio stations.

Isolation runs until σ falls to this value, and it waits while shared structure is active. 0 keeps it on through the last step; it doesn't turn isolation off. Raise this to give the last steps back to the whole clip, which smooths the joins.

Velocity runs each lane as its own pass per step and blends them by your curves, so a lane at 0 is off at that moment.
Layer is the older one-pass method, kept for comparison. The LoRA leaks across the clip (a lane at 0 still measured 60-80% LoRA), and it takes one lane with no lane prompt.

What the lanes do
Each lane runs as its own pass with its LoRA, on its own prompt if it has one. The base pass uses the base prompt. At each moment the result is base + Σ amount × (lane − base), so an amount of 1 is the LoRA at its set strength, and lanes that overlap add up instead of crossfading. The blending options are under Advanced.
